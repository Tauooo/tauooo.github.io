Personal project · Everyday engineering · 2025

A little sunshine.
A little water.

I set up a solar-powered irrigation system for my balcony garden so my plants would not have to go without water while I was away for the summer. It brings a simple engineering idea into everyday life: use locally available solar energy and a water reservoir to deliver water to the planters without relying on someone being home.

01 / The problem

Watering when no one is home.

Leaving plants unattended for an extended period presents a practical problem: they still need water, and someone may not always be available to water them. I used a compact solar-powered irrigation setup rather than relying on daily manual watering.

01 · EnergyA small photovoltaic panel supplies energy for the watering system.
02 · StorageA repurposed container holds water to be delivered through tubing.
03 · DeliveryDrip lines lead into the soil of the two planters.

Conceptual system overview based on the photographed setup; not an electrical or plumbing schematic.

02 / From installation to flowers

A small garden, a practical solution.

The later flower photograph is a personal record of the garden, not a controlled evaluation of irrigation performance. The project is a small example of using engineering to solve a problem in daily life.

The photographs and video document the setup. Watering frequency, reservoir endurance and unattended operating duration were not measured in the supplied materials.
